# The Gig: what the tape allows

Generated by `npx tsx docs/gig/density.ts`. Read straight out of
`src/game/gig/tape.ts` and `src/game/gig/songs.ts` — nothing here is
hand-maintained.

> **Trigger** — one musical-grid event with two outputs: a sound in the mix
> and one attack in the world. Density is triggers per bar. A **bar** is the
> few-second musical measure the loop is built from — and in this branch a bar
> lasts three different lengths, because the three songs run at three tempos.

This is deliberately NOT the same table as
[`docs/baseline/density.md`](../baseline/density.md). That one derives every
density from the shipped
four-rung ladders. Under The Gig nobody authored your pattern, so there is no
density to derive: what is derivable is the **room** the tape gives you, and the
rest has to be measured off played runs and the capture set.

## The tape ladder

| rung | bars | hits (ceiling) | triggers/bar at full | taps this pass asks for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 2 | 2.00 | 2 (a fresh take) |
| 2 | 1 | 3 | 3.00 | 1 |
| 3 | 2 | 7 | 3.50 | 1 |
| 4 | 2 | 9 | 4.50 | 2 |
| 5 | 2 | 11 | 5.50 | 2 |
| 6 | 4 | 23 | 5.75 | 1 |
| 7 | 4 | 26 | 6.50 | 3 |
| 8 | 4 | 28 | 7.00 | 2 |

**No rung thins a track out.** Per-bar density climbs 2.00 → 3.00 → 3.50 → 4.50 → 5.50 → 5.75 → 6.50 → 7.00 and never falls, so a level-up is never a trap. Every fall in density a run
shows is therefore the player's own choice — which is exactly what the hard
floor (#256) asks a stack to be able to produce.

**A track's whole life costs 14 taps**, spread over 8 short passes.
That is the point of copying the existing take into new bars: it keeps this an
overdubbing session rather than a button-mashing test. The one place execution
genuinely costs is a voluntary re-cut at the top, which asks for all 28 in a
single loop.

## The ceiling, against the control

| build | triggers/bar |
|---|---|
| control, `07-maxed-five` (kick, bass, hat, chord, lead) at VIII | 34.00 |
| The Gig, 5 tracks maxed and **every hit budget filled** | 35.00 |
| control, densest 5 at VIII (arp, hat, shaker, chord, chank) | 51.00 |
| control, sparsest 5 at VIII (drone, flute, bell, sub, organ) | 10.75 |

The Gig's ceiling was aimed at the control's reference maxed build (34.00/bar,
the point `docs/baseline/` and `docs/palette-control/` both call `07-maxed-five`)
and it lands just above it. The control can go far denser than either — pick the
five busiest voices and it fires 51.00 a bar — and that ceiling is not the
interesting number anyway.

**The interesting number is the floor.** The control's sparsest maxed build
fires 10.75/bar and that is a floor a player cannot get below: the only lever
on density is refusing level-ups, which costs damage and buys nothing you can
hear. The Gig's floor is **5.00/bar** — one hit a bar per track — and it is
reachable at any moment by re-cutting, for the price of the valley. That gap is
the whole of what this stack is for.

## Where the density can go that the control's cannot

A tape is up to four bars long and the hits may sit anywhere on it, so a build
can put its whole budget in one bar of the phrase. The control cannot: every
authored rung is either one bar (identical every bar) or written to fill its
bars evenly.

| shape of a maxed track | bar 1 | bar 2 | bar 3 | bar 4 | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| spread evenly | 7 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 7.00 |
| front-loaded | 16 | 12 | 0 | 0 | 7.00 |

Same mean, and a completely different four bars. The mean is what a density
table can see; the difference between those two rows is what a loudness meter
sees. `docs/baseline/` measured loudness range at 0.4–0.6 LU at every point and
`docs/palette-control/` measured 0.3–0.7 LU, both concluding there are no peaks
at any scale. **A front-loaded phrase is the cheapest peak this game has ever
had access to**, and whether it clears the 0.6 LU floor is a measurement, not an
argument — see [`README.md`](README.md).

## The set list

| section | from | to | tempo | shuffle | draws heavier on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LOAD-IN (SONG 1 OF 3) | 0:00 | 5:00 | 95 | 0.18 | sub, upright, drone, organ, kick, bass |
| CHANGEOVER | 5:00 | 5:25 | 124 | 0.10 | — |
| THE FLOOR (SONG 2 OF 3) | 5:25 | 10:25 | 124 | 0.10 | kick, clap, snare, bass, chord, hat |
| CHANGEOVER | 10:25 | 10:50 | 140 | 0.00 | — |
| LAST CALL (SONG 3 OF 3) | 10:50 | 15:50 | 140 | 0.00 | hat, shaker, arp, bell, flute, chank, lead |
| ENCORE (THE ENCORE) | 15:50 | 17:20 | 140 | 0.00 | — |

The gig is **17:20** long against the base's 30:00, and it ends on the
encore rather than on a clock.

A bar is a different length in each song, which changes what a take *is* without
the take changing:

| tempo | one bar | a 4-bar loop | a maxed track's triggers/second |
|---|---|---|---|
| 95 | 2.53s | 10.11s | 2.77 |
| 124 | 1.94s | 7.74s | 3.62 |
| 140 | 1.71s | 6.86s | 4.08 |

So the same tape fires 47% more often in song three than in song one. Per-trigger
damage is scaled against tempo (`tempoComp`, docs/plan.md's own rule) so that is
not a free damage buff — but nothing scales the *sound*, which is the point: a
dense take that breathed at 95 is a blur at 140, and on a choking voice it is a
stutter. That is where "genre-shaped strength" comes from here.

## The decision stream, and the back half

Inherited unchanged from the base, deliberately — it is the same bias in all
five round-1 stacks (#265), so it does not corrupt the comparison.

A full build is **5 tracks × 8 rungs = 40 levels**, the same count the base has,
so it completes at the same place in the clock — around minute fourteen, which
#265 measured at 13:27 and 14:42 on two seeds. On a 30-minute run that leaves the
back half with no decision in it. On a 17:20 gig it leaves the last three or
four minutes.

**And The Gig has something to do there that the base does not.** A re-cut is
free and always available, so a maxed build's remaining decision is not *what to
take* but *what to play* — which is the only answer to the dead back half that
costs no new content.
